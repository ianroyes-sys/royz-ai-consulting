# Royz Creative: paid pilot delivery

## Sell one unit

One specialty-coffee SKU, three independently motivated concepts, two sizes per concept (1080×1080 and 1080×1350), six PNGs, one consolidated revision round. $350 one time. Three business days after complete assets, signed-off scope and verified payment; confirm an actual date before collecting payment. No automatic recurring charge. Video and localization are proposal-only until separately validated.

## Intake and acceptance

Collect a product page, high-resolution approved pack shot (prefer transparent PNG), logo, brand colors/fonts and license constraints, exact copy, approved claims, target audience, placement, examples, exclusions, final approver and asset-use permission. Ask only for missing inputs. Do not substitute invented reviews for actual customer language. Read actual reviews before attributing an angle to them.

Accept only a scope the current production method can fulfill. A page URL is evidence, not approval to reproduce every asset. Treat labels, logos and claims as exact requirements. No inferred discounts, health claims, donation figures, or dates. Reject deceptive testimonials and unsupported guarantees. Confirm whether any reference material may be processed with external AI tools.

## Production recipe

1. Write twenty short angle candidates using approved facts: ritual, taste, brewing method, occasion, gifting, product detail. Record the factual source for each. Select three different arguments, not three colors of the same ad.
2. Draft exact copy and layout. In a coding session, use the built-in image generator for approved new visual exploration; do not claim that this is the app's automated Higgsfield connection.
3. Prefer a supplied unchanged product cutout for final composition. Generated packaging is a concept, not proof of label accuracy. Keep typography as editable layout text for exact spelling and easy revisions. If generated product pixels drift, restore the approved photograph or ask for suitable source assets; do not wave it through QA.
4. Export square and portrait separately, retaining safe margins and readable copy. The reusable example renderer is `node scripts/creative-assets.mjs`; its fictional Daybreak photograph and copy must be replaced for client work, never reused as a real product.
5. Inspect every file at full size and phone size against the brief. Confirm all six dimensions, filenames, product identity, packaging, exact text, color, CTA, claims, rights, and no unwanted watermark. Check that source images have not been stretched or unintentionally cropped. Save the checklist with the job.
6. Human delivery approval is required. Share six final files, a file manifest, concept rationale and testing suggestions. Do not claim performance until the client supplies measured results.

## Revisions, records, and delivery

One consolidated revision within the approved direction. Record request, affected files, remedy, expected time/cost and whether it changes scope. Correct our specification mistakes without counting them as the included creative revision. Get a change order for new SKUs, new concepts or added formats. Retain original and revised versions separately. Archive files in agreed durable storage; a provider output URL with a seven-day retention window is not an archive.

Use names such as `brand_sku_angle01_square_v01.png`. Deliver an accompanying manifest with pixel dimensions, approved copy, source rights, date, file hash and approval. Keep client materials outside `public/`. The project excludes `docs/` from Vercel uploads; private prospect examples live there, not under the public site.

## Economics

Treat channel fees, payment fees, production usage, contractor costs and time as different expenses. Do not call client price minus image cost net profit. Enter actual fees from the transaction. Labor and overhead remain costs even when no new software is purchased. Until a funded provider budget is explicitly approved, automatic generation spend is $0. Missing catalog pricing must trigger review, not a made-up quote.

Initial capacity assumption: at most two concurrent paid pilots until actual time per job is measured. Do not sell a faster deadline without checking workload. Log hours spent so the next quote reflects reality. After five paid pilots, assess conversion, revision burden, delivery time and contribution after time before offering weekly subscriptions.

## Communications

Intake: “Thanks—before I scope this, I need [specific missing items]. The pilot is $350 once for three concepts in two sizes, with one revision.”

Scope: “For [SKU], we will produce [exact six-file list] using [approved assets and claims]. Initial delivery is [confirmed date] after payment and complete inputs. One revision round is included. [Exclusions]. Please approve this scope in your private workspace.”

Progress: “The three directions are [rationales]. Please confirm [actual decision]. [Specific label/copy issue] is still under review.”

Delivery: “Here are the six approved files and manifest. Each concept is in square and 4:5. The angle notes explain what each tests. These are creative hypotheses, with no performance results yet. Send one consolidated revision list by [date].”

Expansion: “After you have results from the pilot, we can scope another batch around what you learned. There is no automatic renewal.”

## Learning loop

After each job record: missing inputs, chosen angle, approved source claims, model/tool route, estimated versus actual hours/spend, label errors, revisions, client preferences and observed campaign results (if supplied). Carry forward only account-specific preferences with their source. Never turn an old likeness/voice permission into new consent.
